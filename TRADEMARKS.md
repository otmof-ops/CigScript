# Trademark policy

**CigScript** and the CigScript logo (when there is one) are trademarks of Jay
Taylor. The code is Apache-2.0 and the Apache licence says so itself in its
section 6: it grants no trademark rights. This page says what that means in
practice, so nobody has to guess.

The short version: use the name to say what your thing is made of or works
with; do not use it to say what your thing *is*.

## Free to do, no permission needed

- Say that software is **written in CigScript**, **runs CigScript scripts**,
  **is compatible with CigScript** or **is based on CigScript**, when that is
  accurate.
- Implement the language. The keywords (`roll`, `burn`, `stick`, `exhale` and
  the rest), the grammar and the standard library names are not claimed as
  trademarks or as protected expression; anyone may write a CigScript
  interpreter, compiler, formatter, editor plugin or teaching material.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, reviews and
  criticism, including unflattering ones.
- Distribute unmodified official builds under the CigScript name, with the
  NOTICE file intact, as Apache-2.0 requires.
- Name a package, plugin or repository in a way that describes its
  relationship truthfully, such as `cigscript-vscode`, `cig-mode` or
  `awesome-cigscript`, provided it is clear the project is not the official one.

## Ask first

- Naming a **fork, product, service, company or domain** "CigScript" or a
  confusingly similar name (Cigscript, CigScripts, CygScript, cigscript.dev).
  Forks are welcome under Apache-2.0; they need their own name once they
  differ from the official builds.
- Using the **logo** anywhere other than to link to the official project.
- Calling a build, distribution or service **official**, **certified**, or
  otherwise implying endorsement.
- Merchandise carrying the name or logo.

Write to the address in `SECURITY.md` with what you have in mind. Answers are
usually quick, and the usual answer to a truthful, non-confusing use is yes.

## House style, which keeps the mark a mark

- CigScript is one word, capital C, capital S. Use it as an adjective for a
  thing ("the CigScript runtime", "a CigScript script"), not as a verb or a
  generic noun for scripting languages.
- The command is `cig`; the file extension is `.cig`. Neither is claimed as
  a mark.
- The first prominent use in a document may carry ™ (or ® once a registration
  exists; none is claimed as registered today). After that, plain text.

## Why this exists

Apache-2.0 lets anyone copy, change and sell the code. It does not let anyone
sell it as CigScript, or pass their own work off as this project, and the
Apache NOTICE requirement keeps the author's credit on every copy. This
policy is the part of that arrangement Apache leaves to the trademark
owner. It is written to be easy to comply with, and it is not a licence to
the software itself: that is `LICENSE`.
